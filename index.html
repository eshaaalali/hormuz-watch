Hormuz Watch

Daily vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz, charted against Brent crude, over a rolling 77-day window. Roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes through this 33 km gap between Iran and Oman — when transits fall, this is where it shows first.

    Sources

    • Vessel transits: IMF PortWatch, Daily Chokepoint Transit Calls (chokepoint6 — Strait of Hormuz), AIS-derived, IMF & University of Oxford Environmental Change Institute. PortWatch flags reduced reliability during the current conflict — GPS jamming, AIS spoofing and vessels going dark all suppress the count below actual traffic, so read these as a floor, not a ceiling.
    • Brent crude price: FRED series DCOILBRENTEU (Europe Brent Spot Price FOB, $/barrel), sourced from the US Energy Information Administration. Weekends/holidays have no quote, so those dates are simply skipped rather than estimated — every point on this page is a real reported figure.

    Notes

    Transit counts are IMF PortWatch's AIS-based estimate of vessels passing through the chokepoint boundary that day, across all cargo types (tanker, dry bulk, container, general cargo, ro-ro). "Tankers" is the subset identified as tanker-type vessels. Correlation is computed on the window shown, price vs. total transits, and is not evidence of causation on its own.

    How I built this

    I put this together to see whether Hormuz traffic and oil price actually move together, day by day, rather than just arguing about it. Everything here runs on free, public sources and tools — no paid data, no backend, no database — just a scheduled script re-pulling official IMF and US EIA figures and committing the result.
